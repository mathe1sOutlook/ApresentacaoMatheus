Real challenges

Does your operation recognise itself here?

Common situations in companies that want to grow but hit obstacles in practice. If any of these feels familiar, you are not alone.

Marketing

Money goes into marketing every month. Nobody can say for sure which action brought which sale.

  • Campaign, content, event: all running, none tracked through to the sale
  • Next month's budget decided the same way as last year's: on gut feeling
Processes

The company grew. The processes are still the ones from when it was small.

  • If that person goes on holiday, nobody else knows how it works
  • Each team with its own spreadsheet, its own way, its own version of the truth
Communication

The website says one thing. The salesperson says another. The sales material says a third.

  • A sales proposal that looks like another company's, next to the website
  • The client leaves the meeting unsure of what they are buying
Data

Two spreadsheets, two different numbers for the same indicator. Nobody knows which one to trust.

  • Each team closes its own report before looking at the others'
  • The decision is made, and only later someone notices the number changed
Technology

The company bought the right tool. The team keeps working as if it did not exist.

  • A side spreadsheet to do what the system would already do on its own
  • Nobody was properly trained, so nobody trusts the system
Continuity

The project was delivered. Nobody has been back since.

  • A bug reported months ago, still on the same list
  • The vendor who delivered it no longer picks up the phone
Research

The company decides positioning, campaigns and even pricing on the gut feeling of whoever is in the room. Nobody asked the market first.

  • A competitor launches something similar and the company is caught off guard
  • A campaign that looked good internally converts nobody
Applied AI

Someone on the team spends hours reading contracts, spreadsheets or reports by hand. That could be automatic.

  • Expensive people's time spent on tasks that should belong to a machine
  • Human error in something repetitive that should never depend on a human

Beyond dashboards: models that reveal patterns a standard report does not show. Technology & Data

AI applied to the business

Forecasting and decisions with models chosen by the problem, not by fashion. Radial basis function networks (RBF) to estimate sales, costs and cash flow from history; incremental learning (ART) for operations whose data changes continuously; and associative memory (Hopfield) to reconstruct incomplete patterns. Always with accuracy measured on your own data — not promises.

You receive
Model in use in the operation, continuously evaluated, with an accuracy report.

FundaCalc

No software did this when it launched

FundaCalc

Structural engineering · master’s project · Matheus

Problem

Pile caps are complex elements in structural engineering: they carry the load from the columns to the foundation piles, sharing the forces of the superstructure among them.

That transfer is not uniform, and it grows more complex with multiple columns, eccentricities and imperfections in how the piles are positioned and driven. The few cases backed by experimental tests generally start from idealised geometries, far from many real design and construction situations.

Solution

FundaCalc turns that geometry into a computational model. The cap is discretised and analysed with finite elements, which makes it possible to represent different supports, loads, eccentricities and arrangements of columns and piles.

On that same basis, different analysis and design methods can be applied and compared on the same geometry. During the research, no commercial tools with this purpose were found in Brazil.

Value delivered

FundaCalc brings the calculation model closer to real design and construction conditions.

The engineer can represent more complex geometries, see how the forces are transferred and compare different methods on the same problem, making an analysis that is hard to carry out by hand clearer, verifiable and consistent.

Method

  • Truss assembled by finite elements
  • Statically admissible solution — lower bound theorem
  • Eccentricity in the column and in the pile
  • More than one column over the same cap
  • Born as a Python notebook, then .NET MAUI desktop, today in the browser
